\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{What the trigger buys}
The equal-budget controls answer the question of the title. With a few thousand labels, buying them in a burst after a label-free alarm beats spreading them uniformly, choosing uncertain messages or buying at random times, for both learners and under all four stream designs, and it comes within {{=abs([vs|Budget-Oracle|d]):.3f}} MCC of an oracle that knows where the segments begin. What the trigger does not buy is the accuracy of full supervision. The online TF-IDF learner with every label is {{=-[vs|Online-TFIDF-SGD|d]:.3f}} MCC better on the default stream, and its advantage sits in the first batches of each new LLM segment, where it learns from every label while SLIDE still scores with the previous head. Which policy is right depends on the price and the delay of a label. If every message is verified within one or two batches, full supervision is the better choice; if verification takes eight batches or more, the two end at the same MCC (Section~\ref{sec:delay}) and SLIDE needs {{=[main|SLIDE|labels]/[main|Online-TFIDF-SGD|labels]:.1%}} of the labels.

The revision also changes what we claim about the update. The four-term objective of the earlier design adds nothing to stream accuracy, and the surprisal features add nothing at all. Replay matters for a different reason: it raises the end-of-stream detection rate on earlier campaigns from {{ret|SLIDE|end:.3f}} to {{ret|SLIDE+replay|end:.3f}} (Table~\ref{tab:rev-retention}) at a small cost in current accuracy. Prequential MCC on a stream that never returns to an old source cannot show this, so a stream benchmark for this setting should report retention next to stream accuracy.

\subsection{Deployment considerations}
SLIDE fits a gateway as an asynchronous side-car: the drift test runs once per batch on cached vectors and an alarm becomes a labelling ticket for the last 500 messages. Three operating rules follow from Section~\ref{sec:results}. First, the reference must be rebuilt from post-update traffic only, or the test compares the new head's regime with the old one and alarms again. Second, the label-free trigger needs a small labelled audit stream next to it if posterior changes matter; Section~\ref{sec:realdrift} shows that such an audit must be larger than five or ten labels per batch to be useful. Third, windowed scoring defeats benign padding but should be applied selectively, because on ordinary traffic it raised the false-positive rate from {{main|SLIDE|fpr:.3f}} to {{chunk|SLIDE chunked|fpr:.3f}}. The cost of the frozen encoder is the main computational argument against SLIDE where a TF-IDF detector is enough (Section~\ref{sec:cost}).

\subsection{Threats to validity}\label{sec:validity}
\paragraph{Construct validity} The human corpora label spam and phishing together, so the positive class is \emph{malicious} mail and the human part of the stream is not a pure phishing benchmark; the LLM segments contain phishing only. LLM-written legitimate mail is separable from human mail by style ({{auth_probe:.4f}} macro-F1), and the transformer heads flag {{slice|SLIDE|D_llm|fpr:.3f}} of the LLM-written legitimate messages in segment~D, against {{slice|Online-TFIDF-SGD|D_llm|fpr:.3f}} for the online TF-IDF learner. Part of what the head learns in segment~D is therefore authorship rather than intent. Segment~F, where legitimate and malicious LLM mail are equally frequent, reduces but does not remove this concern.

\paragraph{Internal validity} Hyper-parameters were fixed before the stream runs and none was tuned on stream data, which also holds for the baseline detectors (ADWIN and KSWIN with the settings of Table~\ref{tab:hyper}); tuned baselines might narrow or widen the gaps. Ten realisations limit the smallest attainable Holm-adjusted $p$ to {{p_min_holm:.4f}}, and the sensitivity grids use five, so their differences below about 0.01 MCC are not resolved. The equal-budget samplers spend the labels of SLIDE on average, not per realisation.

\paragraph{External validity} The stream is a composition of public corpora, not the traffic of one organisation, and each boundary changes the source corpus as well as the campaign. The gradual, variable-length and permuted designs reduce, but do not remove, the dependence on this construction. Three LLM releases and one T5 paraphraser cover few generators; GPTGen was written with OpenAI models, and a generator--critic attacker that queries the detector \cite{qi2025} was not studied. The real-drift probe is synthetic: it flips the labels of one region of legitimate mail, which tests the blind spot of label-free detection but not how often such changes occur in practice. Label noise in the purchased windows was not studied.
